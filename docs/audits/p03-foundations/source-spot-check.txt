The purpose of the Cross Curves is to be able to make static stability curves for the ship in any sailing 
condition. 
 
Example: Steel coils departure condition (see chapter 15):  
Displacement : 13201.307 ton 
Trim   :-0.774 m 
GM’      :  3.005 m    
VCG'    :  4.647 m (corrected for free surface moments). 
 
The calculation of righting levers is as follows: 
G'Nsinφ  =  KNsin φ - VCG'sinφ where: 
G'Nsinφ  =  actual righting lever (GZ). 
KNsinφ   =  cross curve ordinate for this displacement which can be found in the cross curve  
  tables. 
VCG' =  KG’ = centre of gravity corrected for free surface effects. 
φ =  angle of inclination.

By erecting the G'M at 1 rad (57.3 deg) and connecting this point to the origin, the tangent of the 
curve is obtained. 
G'M  =  KM - VCG' where: 
G'M  =  Metacentric height corrected for free surface effects. 
KM   =  Vertical distance between metacentre and baseline, which can be found in the tables 
  for hydrostatic particulars. 
VCG' =  Centre of gravity corrected for free surface effects.
